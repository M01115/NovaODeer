A draft typeface for Puff. The skeleton and weight come from Duolingo Sans: one heavy weight, soft-cornered ends, a big x-height and tight spacing. The surface comes from Puff's drawings: gouache edges cut by hand, letters that roll a little off the baseline, and spike-shaped dots on the i and j.

From Duolingo Sans

  • One heavy weight, built as a single stroke. Ends are squared off with softened corners, a little firmer than Duolingo's fully round ends.
  • Big x-height (54 of a 74 cap) and short descenders, so words stay chunky at small sizes.
  • Bowls are squarer than circles, which gives the bouncy, toy-like feel.
  • Double-story a, l with a small foot, t with a hooked tail.
  • Tight spacing: one unit of tracking.

From Puff's drawings

  • No outline. Puff is flat gouache, so the letters are filled shapes too.
  • Hand-cut edge: slow, wide waves push every edge a little, the way a brush shape wobbles. No fine jitter.
  • Roll: each letter tips up to a couple of degrees, like Puff rolling on land.
  • The dots on i and j are Puff's spikes: a tilted wedge, not a circle.
  • Puff style copies the body: orange rim, yellow top, cream belly below the middle line.

What this draft is

Each glyph is a centerline path drawn with a round stroke, then roughened. That is quick to change while the shapes settle. To use it in the Expo app it needs to become a real font file: outline the strokes, bake one fixed wobble, and export an .otf that expo-font can load. Duolingo Sans itself is a licensed typeface, so this borrows its proportions and habits, not its outlines.
